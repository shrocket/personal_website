I read this book on my phone. The file I have is a .mobi file, which is basically a deprecated file format when it comes to eBooks. So I had to find an eReader app that could deal with such files. Furthermore, the file I have was an imperfect OCR scan of a physical book and so maybe the scan was only >90% "correct," which meant I had to read around a lot of scanning errors, and so maybe I missed something important in book because I misinterpreted-- but that's always a danger, regardless of imperfect OCR scanning. And, you know, _The Man in the High Castle_ is, in part, about a book that describes an alternate reality (which just happens to be the historical reality in which I read the book and you are reading this blog post).

In any case, the book is a typical Dickian layering of realities extending past the boundaries of the physical book and into the reader's reality, so I feel as though the way I read it was appropriate.

On what my eReader-app is telling me was page 95, I marked the following bit of text:

> Ideological orientation suggesting medieval Jesuitic viewpoint exacerbated by post-Romantic Germanic nihilism.

I feel as though this is an apt description of current German mentality, i.e. the water in which I daily bathe.

And on "page" 223, I marked this:

> Like lethal astigmatism striking without warning.

This sentence pretty much sums up PKD's oeuvre.

Otherwise, I don't really know. I suppose I should write about my own use of the _I Ching_ and other divinatory devices, but I don't really know how Dick put the _I Ching_ to work for him, nor do I fully understand why the climax of this book is the titular man in the high castle being outed and shamed for having used the _I Ching_ to write his book.

Or I suppose I could write about the idea in theoretical physics that multiverse is real.

Or I could write about how I asked co-pilot (not because it was the one best suited to the task, but because it was the one within easiest reach) to think about the phrase "lethal astigmatism" and how it introduced me to the [SCP Foundation](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/), which is like the bastard love child of H.P. Lovecraft and Philip K. Dick . . .

But

I read the book, and it is a typical Dickian layering of realities extending past the boundaries of the physical book and into the mind/reality of the reader something something _paranoia_ something something _gnosis_
