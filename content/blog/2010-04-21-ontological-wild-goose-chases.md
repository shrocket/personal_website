When I was active in the theatre, I got very upset when my fellow actors would simply deliver the essence of a line, and not the line as it was written. My thought process was that the playwright had spend a great deal of time and intellectual energy crafting that line just so, and it was the actor's job to be faithful to all that work. This is one of the reasons why I am now a writer. However, since I started writing, I have learned that there are different kinds of writing and different kinds of reading. That sounds too easy. It is. The point is this: I know that no reader is going to put the same amount of time and effort into my writing as I did. I cannot control the reader. So, dear Reader, please feel free to read this in any way you like, whether that's all the way from the beginning to the last word in order, or a couple of words or sentences here and there in whatever order you see fit.

That being said, writers often talk about how a text teaches the reader how to read it in the first few pages, sometimes even sentences. Of course, knowing how something wants to be read isn't the same as reading or having read the whole thing. Oversimplified, this boils down to form vs. content, and that's not, so far as I know here at the beginning, what I want to talk about in this post.

I did not read as much of Gunther Kress' book [_Multimodality: A social semiotic approach to contemporary communication_](http://www.amazon.com/Multimodality-Semiotic-Approach-Contemporary-Communication/dp/0415320607/ref=sr_1_3?ie=UTF8&s=books&qid=1271882508&sr=8-3 "I did not pay this much for my copy") as I would have liked. I read more than half of it, and I read the first chapter carefully. I may not know everything, content-wise, that Mr. Kress wanted me to know, but, thanks to the form, I think I have a good handle on where he was going.

I am not going to attempt to draw a map. No, wait. I am not going to attempt to draw a map of the parts of the book I didn't read. Instead, I am going to make a map of the first chapter—an annotative map. However, there won't be a traditional key, and the water may or may not be blue. . . .
