## Sidebar

On the penultimate page of Alice Notley's epic poem _Disobedience_, she writes, 

> "Engaging" is an asshole word, / not quite as obnoxious a cliché as "ground-breaking."

The first sentence of the blurb on the back of Notley's book reads,

> Paris-based Alice Notley has earned a reputation as one of the most challenging and _engaging_ poets at work today \[emphasis mine]

I think I hate the word "abound."

## Continuation

The phrase "tour de force" appears on the back of Dash Shaw's 2008 book _Bottomless Belly Button_, and maybe it is that. I know what the French means, but I don't really know what any words in blurbs actually mean; blurbs are a kind of purely superficial language: they make the sounds we expect them to make, but mean nothing. Nobody buys a book based on the blurb? 

The word "McGuffin" appears nowhere either in or on the book, but the story is full of them:
- the three keys (unless the fourth key gives the first three meaning)
- the drawing of the stairs
- the secret passageways
- the balloon chair
- the tooth
- the divorce itself (?)

If a McGuffin is a device that serves merely as a trigger for the plot, what is the plot of _Bottomless Belly Button_? What are all these McGuffins triggering?

Very quotidian, very normal, very very beautiful insight and change. That's all.

_Bottomless Belly Button_ is the story of a family falling apart.

1. All families are falling apart.
2. All stories are about families falling apart.
3. "Any minor world that breaks apart falls together again."

I think my favorite panel was the one toward the end of part 2, where the reader finally sees what Peter _actually_ looks like instead of what Peter _thinks_ he looks like.

I think I like Peter's story best. But I liked all the characters and their stories.

Ultimately, I found Dash Shaw's _Bottomless Belly Button_ highly engaging.
